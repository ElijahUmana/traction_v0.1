WHY A DIRECTORY AND NOT AN ENV VAR
  main.db          honours JAC_DATA_PATH   runtimelib/impl/server.impl.jac:16
  users.db         honours JAC_DATA_PATH   scale/identity/impl/user_manager.impl.jac:37
  anchor_store.db  IGNORES it - hard-coded '.jac/data/anchor_store.db',
                   scale/config/impl/config_loader.impl.jac:218, no env override
  Setting JAC_DATA_PATH moves users.db and not the anchor store, so the guest
  root is recorded in one file and absent from the other - hand-building the
  divergence that bricks the server. anchor_store.db resolves relative to CWD,
  so only a separate working DIRECTORY isolates anything.

   (captured in the first pass; a python3 process held
    .jac/data/anchor_store.db open, standing in for a `jac run`)

       pid 46216: python3 -c \012import time\012f=open('.jac/data/anchor_store.db','r')\012time.sleep(45)\012
    Two processes on one SQLite anchor store is what corrupts the guest
    root and produces the permanent _lock 500s. Stop them, or serve this
    checkout from its own directory with: ops/serve.sh
restart.sh exit=1  (expect 1 = refused)

   The guard asks lsof who has the store open rather than matching process
   names, which is why it catches `jac run` and `jac test` - the actual
   contention source - and not merely a stray `jac start`.

C. FIRST PASS, same instance, before the guard test: probe(60) OK=60 FAIL=0,

D. WHAT KILLED THE FIRST ATTEMPT AT (A) - worth recording
   The jac process died at 60s with "Killed: 9" and an 81-byte log, and the two
   teammate servers on :8000 and :8123 died in the same instant. Nothing in
   ops/ sends a global SIGKILL any more; something else on the machine ran
   `pkill -9 -f "jac start"`. That is precisely the cross-operator stomping the
   scoped kill was introduced to stop, demonstrated live.

   It also exercised the new fail-fast path, which reported
     "!! the jac process is gone - it died at launch, it is not compiling
        (log is /tmp/traction-8877.log, 81 bytes)"
   instead of sitting out the remaining 240s looking like a slow compile.

E. HONEST WALL-CLOCK
   Boot reported 31s / 63s / 93s / 124s and served at 126s. The previous loop
   counted iterations, not seconds, and each pass cost a curl timeout plus a
   sleep - so its "300s" ceiling was really up to 30 minutes and every progress
   line understated elapsed time about 6x.
